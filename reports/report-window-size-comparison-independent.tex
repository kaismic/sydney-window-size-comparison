\begin{abstract}
This study compared 12 window lengths from 20 to 240 seconds for Sydney bus,
car, and train classification. A fixed XGBoost protocol evaluated the same
149 sessions and seven held-out participants at every length. Mean pooled
macro F1 across three seeds peaked at 0.693 for 120-second windows. The result
was descriptive: the windows overlapped, their counts differed by length, and the
length with the highest score was identified on this cohort.
\end{abstract}

\section{Study design}
\subsection{Cohort}
The source was a frozen 166-session Sydney snapshot. Bus, car, and train
recordings with snapshot duration of at least 240 seconds were candidates;
there was no upper-duration limit. Event-data checks and feature-quality rules
required each included session to have at least one valid window at every
tested length. The resulting common cohort had 149 sessions from seven
participants: 38 bus, 66 car, and 45 train. Session identities and
participant folds remained the same across lengths. Selection for long,
quality-surviving sessions limited the population to which these estimates
applied.

\subsection{Windows and features}
Window lengths were 20, 40, \ldots, 240 seconds. Each advanced by half its
length, giving 50\% overlap within a session. For a window of length $W$
seconds, the feature pipeline required at least $30W$ accelerometer, $4W$
gyroscope, and $2W$ magnetometer samples. A required sensor stream with a
consecutive-sample gap over 500 ms caused rejection. The fixed 11-feature
vector contained mean, standard deviation, range, and minimum of
accelerometer and gyroscope magnitudes, plus standard deviation, range, and
minimum of magnetometer magnitude. Sensor inputs, feature definitions,
quality rules, and overlap fraction were held fixed across lengths.

\subsection{Training and scoring}
For each length, leave-one-participant-out evaluation trained on six
participants and tested on the seventh. The XGBoost hyperparameters were
selected on a separate NOR-TMD dataset and remained fixed here. Each fold was
trained only on Sydney windows, using class-balanced training weights.
Seeds 42, 43, and 44 gave 36 length-and-seed evaluations and 252 model fits
across the seven folds. No held-out participant contributed training windows
to its fold.

Within each length and seed, predictions from all seven held-out folds were
pooled. Macro F1 was calculated as the mean of bus, car, and train F1;
balanced accuracy was calculated as the mean of their recalls. Reported scores
were means of the three seed-level pooled metrics. Each session was tested
once per seed and length, but shorter lengths produced more overlapping
windows. Pooled scores therefore weighted sessions by their retained window
counts. They were neither participant-equal
scores nor measures from independent journeys.

\section{Results}
Mean macro F1 rose from 0.596 at 20 seconds to 0.693 at 120 seconds, then
varied from 0.662 to 0.682 across the remaining lengths. Balanced accuracy
followed the same broad pattern and reached 0.691 at 120 seconds.

retained window counts as length increased.

\section{Interpretation and limitations}
The 120-second length had the highest observed mean macro F1 in this grid.
It required 120 seconds of sensor history for a first full-window prediction
and advanced in 60-second steps. No on-device latency, energy use, or
transition-detection delay was measured, so the operational trade-off could
not be quantified from these scores.

The seven participants, class concentration, shared sessions across lengths,
and window-level pooling limited generalization beyond this cohort. Feature
definitions were informed by earlier Sydney exploration, although model
hyperparameters were selected on separate data. The peak was selected after
examining all 12 lengths; it did not establish a confirmed optimum. No
independent participant cohort with the length fixed in advance was available
to assess whether the observed peak persisted.
