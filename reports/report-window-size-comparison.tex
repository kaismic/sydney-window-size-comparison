\section*{Summary}
The highest mean macro F1 in this study was 0.693 at a 120-second window, versus
0.643 at the 60-second reference setting. The mean paired difference was +0.050,
but its bootstrap 95\% interval (\(-0.008\) to +0.087) includes zero. The
120-second setting is the best observed choice in this grid; these results do
not establish a clear improvement over 60 seconds under the study's resampling
scheme.

\section*{Study design}
The frozen Sydney cohort contains 149 bus, car, and train sessions from seven
participants. Window lengths range from 20 to 240 seconds in 20-second steps,
with a step of half the window length. A fixed XGBoost model configuration was
evaluated with seven leave-one-participant-out folds and three random seeds
(42, 43, and 44), giving 36 size-and-seed runs and 252 model fits. Scores are
pooled over held-out windows for each seed and then averaged across seeds.
All window sizes use the same sessions, although the number of windows changes
with window length.

\section*{Interpretation}
The 120-second window has the strongest observed average score and improves
bus F1 from 0.426 at 60 seconds to 0.540. Its interval against the reference
crosses zero, so the observed advantage is uncertain. The results are limited
to this frozen cohort, fixed model, and window-level evaluation; longer
windows also yield fewer evaluated examples per session.
